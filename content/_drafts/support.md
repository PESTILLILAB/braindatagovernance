Global Brain Governance is funded by Wellcome through BRIDGE. The first product is out. The next ones are scoped, and each needs a partner: a funder, an institution with legal expertise in a region we do not yet cover, or a repository willing to adopt what we build.
{: .lead}

## What the current funding has produced

The Wellcome grant to BRIDGE paid for the legal analysis of 93 countries, the Neurodata Governance Toolkit and its modular agreement, three community workshops on three continents, and this site. The analysis and the toolkit are under peer review; the Legal landscape explorer and the toolkit are already open for anyone to use.

## Phase 1: what we will demonstrate

The work runs in phases with stated goals ([Phase 1](/about/#phase) runs 2026–2029). Each package below is a product on the [roadmap](/products/#roadmap), stated as something a funder or partner could take on; each is demonstrated by member institutions before release. Costs and durations are indicative; we are glad to scope any of them in detail.

::cards wide
- **Demonstrations and training** {#pilots} — Running real collaborations through the toolkit with their legal, ethics and repository offices, and teaching data stewards and ethics committees to use it. Needs: three to five collaborations, travel and facilitation, 12 months. Delivers: evaluation evidence, revised tools, trained people.
- **ezGov** {#ezgov} — An AI-assisted guide that takes a collaboration through the toolkit in plain language, looks up the law at both ends and drafts the governance record and the agreement modules for the institutions to review. Needs: 18 months of engineering and legal review, and three pilot collaborations across regions. Delivers: a working guide, piloted, with the records it produced.
- **Legal landscape handbook** {#handbook} — A country profile for each of the 93 jurisdictions, with the instruments, dates and the reasoning behind each pathway class, kept current by regional reviewers. Needs: legal time in each region and an editor; 12 months for the first edition. Delivers: the reference researchers, ethics committees and data-protection officers ask for first.
- **New regions** {#regions} — Asia, Oceania and the Middle East are not yet covered. Needs: legal scholars in each region, the coding method we already have, and a year per region. Delivers: a global map rather than a three-continent one.
- **Machine-readable governance** {#labels} — Governance labels for datasets and derivatives that repositories and platforms can read, so that conditions travel with the data. Needs: a standards partner (we work with INCF and build on existing data-use ontologies), two repositories willing to adopt, 18 months. Delivers: a published specification with reference implementations.
- **Institutional governance profiles** {#profiles} — A standard profile per institution and repository, so that partners stop answering the same due-diligence questions for every collaboration. Needs: ten member institutions to pilot the profile and a host; 12 months. Delivers: the profile standard and the first directory.
- **Registry of models trained on brain data** {#registry} — A schema and a reference registry recording provenance, training-data lineage, intended use and inference risk for models trained on neural data. Needs: a technical partner and a host; 12 months. Delivers: the piece of the ecosystem that follows data into AI.

## How the work is sustained

The site, the data and the code are open (CC BY 4.0 and MIT) and hosted on GitHub, so nothing depends on a single server or a single grant to stay available. BRIDGE, powered by INCF, provides the community process and the regional reviewers. The Pestilli Laboratory at The University of Texas at Austin maintains the products. Legal content carries a date and is reviewed by a scholar from its region before release, which is the recurring cost that funding keeps alive.
