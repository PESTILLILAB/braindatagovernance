Neurodata Governance Toolkit

Decision support for sharing neural data across borders. Version 1.1, October 2026.

Legal landscape ↗

Four tools that take a collaboration from “what are these data?” to the clauses its agreement needs.

  • Tool 1Classify. What can the data reveal or enable, for this recipient? Tool 1 assigns a tier by functional exposure.
  • Tool 2Test the pathway. Can the proposed transfer, access or public release carry the safeguards that tier requires? Tool 2 decides: proceed, proceed after safeguards, redesign, or do not proceed.
  • Tool 3Reassess derivatives and models. Features, embeddings, synthetic data and model weights are reassessed for identification, inference, persistence and leakage before they circulate. Tool 3 retains the tier, refers to Tier 4 or 5, or stops.
  • Tool 4Select the clauses. Tool 4 maps the results to the modules of the Modular Neurodata Transfer and Access Agreement (MDTAA).

Built from the law of 93 countries in Africa, Europe, and Latin America and the Caribbean. The tools structure the questions and record the answers. They do not establish that a transfer is lawful, and they do not replace ethics, repository, institutional or legal review.

1
Tier 1 — Aggregated or functionally non-revealing neurodata
Aggregated, statistical or summary-level information that contains no participant-level record, persistent participant trace or linkage mechanism and presents no reasonable small-group disclosure risk.
  • Group-average brain-activation patterns
  • Aggregated connectivity measures and summary statistics
  • Published statistical results with no participant-level records
  • Sufficiently aggregated outputs from which no individual or small group can reasonably be identified
2
Tier 2 — Pseudonymised or participant-level neurodata without direct identifiers
Participant-level neurodata from which direct identifiers have been removed, whether or not a linkage mechanism is reasonably available to the relevant actor. The assessment records whether linkage is reasonably available to the originating institution, the intended recipient and, for public release, foreseeable downstream users. The tier's minimum measures are the default for controlled access; for public release, Tool 2 Step 8B may accept documented release-specific safeguards that address the same risks.
  • Pseudonymised EEG or MEG data linked to a participant code
  • De-identified MRI capable of re-linkage through a secure institutional key
  • Participant-level processed neurodata held in a controlled repository
  • Coded behavioural-neural datasets
3
Tier 3 — Directly identifiable or intrinsically traceable neurodata
Neurodata containing direct identifiers, recognisable identifying anatomy, identifying acquisition metadata or intrinsic signal characteristics that reasonably permit identification or tracing without relying solely on a separately protected code-to-identity mapping. A raw acquisition format does not, by itself, establish Tier 3.
  • Non-defaced structural imaging; raw DICOM with headers or recognisable anatomy
  • EEG/MEG/ECoG retaining identifying metadata or demonstrated participant-tracing capacity
  • Implant telemetry or BCI recordings with device identifiers or precise timestamps
  • Neurodata associated with names, voice, video or other direct identifiers
4
Tier 4 — Functionally revealing derivative neurodata
Transformed or derived neurodata that may no longer resemble the source dataset but can reasonably reveal, predict, reconstruct or profile neurological, cognitive, emotional or behavioural states.
  • Neural feature vectors, embeddings and neural fingerprints
  • Cognitive or behavioural profiles; classifiers and predictive outputs
  • Reconstructed visual, auditory or cognitive representations
  • Synthetic neurodata or latent representations retaining meaningful neural characteristics
5
Tier 5 — Model-associated or computationally persistent neurodata
Neurodata-derived information associated with an AI model or persistent computational infrastructure where the artefact retains, or may reasonably retain, continuing functional exposure through inference, reconstruction, extraction, profiling or memorisation. Incorporation into a model triggers assessment; it does not by itself establish Tier 5.
  • Model weights trained on neurodata; neuroscience foundation models
  • Federated-learning systems and synthetic-data generators trained on neurodata
  • Persistent embedding repositories
  • AI systems that retain neurodata-derived information after the original research activity
Key principle Tier assignment is informed by functional exposure; legal status remains determined under applicable law. The highest applicable tier governs; safeguards are cumulative; an Unknown answer is governed under the more protective tier until resolved; incorporation into a model triggers assessment but does not by itself establish Tier 5.

Cairo hospital EEG to a London university where the required transfer licence cannot be obtained — do not proceed yet

Cairo University’s neurology department wants to send pseudonymised EEG and clinical data from 300 epilepsy patients to a London university for a genetics-of-epilepsy project. Egypt’s Personal Data Protection Law 151/2020 requires a licence or permit from the Personal Data Protection Centre for cross-border transfer of personal data, and the Executive Regulations issued by Decree 816/2025 set out the licensing procedure. The scenario stipulates that the required licence cannot be obtained for this transfer, so the lawful-pathway check at Tool 2 Step 7 fails; the outcome would change if a licence were granted.
